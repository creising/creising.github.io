Rotary Pi

A very old rotary phone we found in our first house, gutted and rebuilt around a Raspberry Pi 1B and a little OLED screen. Same bells, same dial, brand new brain. Here's how it came together, in four phases.

Phone Found in our first house
Brain Raspberry Pi 1B
Display Small OLED screen

01

Cracking it open

Decoding the dial

First job: figure out how the rotary dial actually talks. The line sits normally closed and opens once per number, so a 1 is one pulse and a 9 is nine. I opened the phone up and wrote a little script to read those pulses and turn them back into digits.

02

First signs of life

Wiring up the receiver

To check the receiver still worked, I clipped some alligator leads onto the guts and ran them to the audio out on the Raspberry Pi, then pushed a few test messages through. Old bells, new brain.

03

The handset

New wire in the handset

The original handset wiring was dry-rotted and dragging down the sound quality, so it got torn out and replaced. A small fix that made a big difference.

04

On the wall

Mounted up

Finally I screwed it to a spare piece of stained plywood from the garage, Raspberry Pi and all, so the whole thing can hang on the wall and actually get used.

05

The finished phone

Back in service

Old dial, old bells, a Raspberry Pi doing the thinking behind them. A classic gem brought back to life.
